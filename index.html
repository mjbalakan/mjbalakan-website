Research

From 2D-material devices to topological superconductors

For more than a decade I have taken devices from idea to measurement: design, cleanroom fabrication, and low-noise electrical characterization from room temperature down to a few kelvin.

2022 – present · SUNY Albany

Topological superconductivity for quantum computing

Topological superconductors can host Majorana modes, the building block of qubits that resist errors by design. I fabricate hBN-encapsulated Fe(Te,Se) rings a few hundred nanometres across and measure them at cryogenic temperatures with low-noise electronics. These devices show half-quantum flux oscillations, coherent edge transport, and a built-in magnetization of the superconducting condensate.

  • Intrinsic magnetization of the superconducting condensate in Fe(Te,Se) (2026)
  • Half-integer winding of order parameter in an iron-based superconductor (2026)
  • Aharonov–Bohm interference from coherent spin-polarized edge transport in Fe(Te,Se) superconducting rings (2025)
  • Observation of half-quantum vorticity in an iron-based superconductor (2025)

Ring device (400 nm scale) · magnetoresistance map at 8 K

2016 – 2021 · University of Tehran

2D materials for CMOS-compatible devices

Combining atom-thin materials with silicon could give chips new functions at lower energy. I measured how charge crosses the graphene–silicon interface, calculated the efficiency limit of graphene solar cells, and grew borophene, a 2D metal for ultrathin interconnects, by chemical vapor deposition for the first time.

  • Chemical vapor deposition of two-dimensional boron sheets by thermal decomposition of diborane (2021)
  • Kinetics of charge carriers across a graphene–silicon Schottky junction (2020)
  • Theoretical efficiency limit of graphene–semiconductor solar cells (2020)
  • Sequentially assembled graphene layers on silicon, the role of uncertainty principles in graphene–silicon Schottky junctions (2019)
  • IR position-sensitive detectors based on double-junction asymmetric TiO₂/MoS₂/reduced graphene-oxide sandwiches (2018)

Graphene–silicon junction: band diagram and in-plane / out-of-plane transport

2018 – 2020 · University of Tehran

Hybrid organic–silicon optoelectronics

Coating silicon with a conductive polymer gives light sensors that are made at low temperature and can run without external power. I worked out how charge moves through these junctions, then built large-area position-sensitive detectors and self-powered photodiodes with a photovoltage responsivity of 10⁶ V/W.

  • Hybrid organic/inorganic position-sensitive detectors based on PEDOT:PSS/n-Si (2018)
  • Mechanism of charge transport in hybrid organic–inorganic PEDOT:PSS/silicon heterojunctions (2019)
  • Ultrahigh photovoltage responsivity of PEDOT:PSS–silicon hybrid heterojunction photodiodes (2020)

PEDOT:PSS / n-Si position-sensitive detector (APL Editor's Pick)

2015 – 2018 · University of Tehran

Solar cells, nanostructures and transport modeling

My early work paired nanostructured solar cells and detectors with computer models of how electrons diffuse through disordered materials. Micro-patterning porous TiO₂ guided electrons toward the electrode and made their diffusion length about three times longer.

  • Electron transport in Q2D porous network of titania nanoparticles, incorporating electrical and optical advantages in dye-sensitized solar cells (2016)
  • Monte Carlo random walk simulation of electron transport in confined porous TiO₂ as a promising candidate for photo-electrode of nano-crystalline solar cells (2015)
  • Kinetic Monte Carlo simulation of single-electron multiple-trapping transport in disordered media (2017)
  • Ultra-low intensity UV detection using partitioned mesoporous TiO₂ (2017)
  • Frequency-driven bulk-to-surface transition of conductivity in ZnO nanowires (2018)
