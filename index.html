Research · 2022 – 2026

Topological
superconductivity
for quantum
computing

Topological superconductors combine the quantum coherence of a superconductor with the protection of topology. They can host Majorana modes, the building blocks of qubits that are far less prone to errors. Proving that a material is a topological superconductor is hard, because its exotic states are hidden beneath a superconducting bulk. The iron-based superconductor FeTeSe is one of the most promising candidates. We fabricate fully encapsulated ring devices, a few hundred nanometers across, and probe them with low-noise, phase-sensitive measurements at a few kelvin. A ring architecture forces the superconducting wavefunction to wind around a closed loop, leading to flux quantization inside the ring. Such quantization can reveal the condensate's topological phase, its complex spin texture, and other associated exotic properties.

  • h/eCoherent single-electron interference confined along the device edges

2014 – 2021 · University of Tehran

Earlier: 2D materials and silicon devices

Before quantum devices, I worked on bringing new materials into silicon technology for light sensors, solar cells and future chips, combining device experiments with physical modeling.

2018 – 2021

CMOS-compatible devices from 2D materials

Graphene lets light pass into the silicon beneath it, which makes graphene–silicon junctions attractive for solar cells and detectors. We showed that charge crossing this 2D/3D interface is limited by how slowly electrons leave the graphene plane, and calculated the efficiency limit of graphene–silicon solar cells. We also grew borophene, a one-atom-thick metal, by chemical vapor deposition, a scalable route to 2D interconnects.

  • 10⁶×slower charge transfer than gold–silicon Schottky contacts
  • 25.5%efficiency limit of graphene/silicon solar cells
  • FirstCVD growth of borophene
2017 – 2020

Hybrid organic–silicon optoelectronics

Coating silicon with a conductive polymer gives junctions that are made at low temperature from solution and work over large areas. We worked out how charge moves through them, injection over an interface barrier in one direction and space-charge-limited flow through the polymer in the other. On this platform we built position-sensitive detectors and self-powered photodiodes that stay stable after long exposure to air.

  • >100 mV/mmposition sensitivity (APL Editor's Pick)
  • 10⁶ V/Wphotovoltage responsivity with pW-level noise
2014 – 2018

Solar cells and physical modeling

Our Monte Carlo simulations showed that confining a porous oxide network steers electrons toward the collecting electrode. We then built column-structured dye-sensitized solar cells and measured longer electron lifetimes and diffusion lengths. With COMSOL and numerical models, we also explained how plasma-enhanced CVD produces self-organized, single-standing carbon nanotubes.

  • ~3×longer electron diffusion length in patterned TiO₂
  • 10⁵×better detection of faint UV light
